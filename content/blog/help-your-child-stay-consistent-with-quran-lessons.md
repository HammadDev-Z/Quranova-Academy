Starting Quran lessons is easy. Keeping them going for months and years is where most families struggle. These ideas help.

## Fix the time and keep it

Children thrive on routine. Choose class days and a time when your child is rested and fed, not just home from a long school day. Keep it the same each week so it becomes automatic.

## Prepare the space

Set up a quiet spot with the device, a mushaf and a notebook ready before class. Remove distractions such as the TV and other devices. A few minutes of preparation prevents a lot of reluctance.

## Be present, especially at the start

Sit with younger children for the first few minutes. Your presence shows the lesson matters. As they grow in confidence, you can step back but stay nearby.

## Praise effort, not perfection

Notice when your child tries hard, even if the recitation is not perfect. Children who feel encouraged keep going; children who feel judged tend to stop.

## Keep daily practice short

Ten minutes a day is far better than a long session the night before class. Link it to something that already happens every day, such as after Maghrib or before bedtime.

## Make progress visible

A simple chart or sticker system helps younger children see how far they have come. Share the monthly progress report with them and celebrate milestones, such as finishing Qaida or a new surah.

## Talk to the teacher

If your child is struggling, tell the teacher. A change in pace, a new approach or a short break can make a big difference. If the match is not working, you can always ask for a different teacher.

## Be patient

There will be weeks when motivation dips. That is normal. What matters is coming back, again and again.

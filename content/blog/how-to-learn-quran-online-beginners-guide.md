Learning the Quran online has become one of the most practical ways to study, especially if there is no qualified teacher near you. Done well, it is just as effective as learning in person. Here is how to start.

## 1. Start with the basics, not the Quran itself

If you or your child cannot yet read Arabic, begin with **Noorani Qaida**. It teaches the letters, their sounds and how they join, so that reading the Quran later is smooth and correct. Skipping this step is the most common reason beginners get stuck.

## 2. Choose a live, one-to-one teacher

Recorded videos are useful for revision, but recitation needs correction in real time. A teacher who listens to you and fixes mistakes as they happen will move you forward far faster than any app. One-to-one lessons also let the teacher work at your pace.

Look for a teacher who:

- Holds a recognised qualification in Quran and tajweed
- Is patient and encouraging, especially with children
- Is available at times that suit your timezone
- Is the gender you are comfortable learning with

## 3. Prepare a simple setup

You do not need much:

- A phone, tablet or computer with a working camera and microphone
- A stable internet connection
- A quiet corner with good lighting
- A mushaf (printed Quran) and a notebook

## 4. Build a routine you can keep

Short, regular lessons beat long, occasional ones. Thirty minutes, a few days a week, with ten minutes of daily practice between lessons, is enough to see steady progress. Pick fixed days and times and treat them like any other appointment.

## 5. Practise between lessons

Lessons show you what to do; practice makes it stick. Re-read what you covered, listen to a reciter you like and recite out loud. Write down questions to ask in your next class.

## 6. Be patient

Everyone starts somewhere. Mistakes are part of learning, and a good teacher expects them. Focus on small, consistent steps, and the progress adds up.
